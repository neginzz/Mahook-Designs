Fixing the Gaps in Goodreads: Designing for Reflection, Discovery, and Connection

UX Design Case Study for iOS mobile app

1.Introduction

2.Overview

This project focuses on improving the user experience of Goodreads. I worked individually, with a research-driven approach to identify and address key user pain points. The goal was to turn user feedback into practical design improvements that would make the iOS mobile app better for readers.

My Role:

This was a solo project completed over the course of five weeks. I was responsible for the end-to-end design process — from defining the problem space to delivering tested solutions.

My work included user research, competitive analysis, user interviews, surveys, synthesizing insights, mapping user flows, ideation, wireframing, designing high-fidelity prototypes, and conducting usability testing and A/B testing.

Tools:

Figma, FigJam, Untitled UI Design System, Typeform

3.Timeline

4.Problem Discovery

As we move forward in a hyper-connected world, people are craving more intentional social spaces.

People don’t just want another social feed—they want a focused space to track their reading and connect with like-minded readers. Existing tools like Goodreads try, but often feel outdated or clunky. What’s missing is a modern, intentional experience that makes it easy and enjoyable to stay organized, inspired, and connected.

How might we design a more thoughtful and inspiring space where readers can reflect on their progress and build connections around books?

10.Key Insights & Pain Points

  • 📌 Users treat Goodreads more as a log than a social space — 85% primarily track their reading.
  • 📌 Discovery is broken — users find it irrelevant, mainstream, and impersonal.
  • 📌 Social features are underused due to poor design, lack of messaging, and confusing mechanics.
  • 📌 Mobile app lags behind web — users prefer desktop for core tasks like filtering or organizing.
  • 📌 Clear need for segmentation — casual readers and power users are poorly served by a one-size-fits-all UX.

17.Usability Testing & A/B Testing

I conducted online moderated Usability Tests and A/B Tests where participants interacted with different flows. I observed their difficulties, gathered feedback, and made improvements based on the findings.

“Add Book” Button Placement

Originally placed as a floating action button on the homepage, users found it inconsistent and confusing—especially since it didn’t appear on other screens. To solve this, I moved the action to the bottom navigation bar, making it always accessible, no matter where users are in the app.

Notifications & Messages Separation

Both notifications and messages were grouped in one space using tabs, but users expressed the need for clearer separation. I split them into two distinct sections and added dedicated icons on the homepage for quicker access and better clarity.

Review & Rating Flow

Initially, users had to write a review on the same page where they rated and added a book. Many found this limiting and preferred more flexibility. I addressed this by creating a dedicated review page with richer tools and more control.

Book Management Section Simplification

  • The book management screen included two top buttons—“Stats” and “Add Book.” Since the "Add Book" function was already available in the main navigation, users found it redundant. I removed the extra button to reduce clutter and improve focus.
  • Sections like “Currently Reading” and the “Reading Challenge” were taking up too much space, making book management feel overwhelming. Users wanted a more compact view, so I reduced their size to improve usability and allow more focus on book lists.

27.Takeaways

Real insights don’t come from assumptions.

I began this project thinking the solution would be mostly visual. But through user interviews, surveys, and real feedback, it became clear: the real challenges were structural, not just aesthetic. This process reminded me how valuable research truly is. Observing how people interact with a product—and listening closely—revealed insights I wouldn’t have uncovered on my own. It also deepened my interest in data and how it can support thoughtful design decisions. Most importantly, I learned the value of involving users early and often. Their input helped me design with more clarity, confidence, and empathy—skills I’ll continue carrying into every project ahead.
